## What v1 got wrong (conclusions only — don't reread the old draft)

**One hard error, proven by the very passage I cited.** v1 named your line-level annotations *tugui* while naming the document *zouchao*. But the *New Tang History*, ch. 47, reads:

"Of all the offices' *zouchao*, once the Chancellor has reviewed them, he **rebuts and corrects errors**. When an **edict** is unsuitable, it is amended and returned — this is called *tugui*."

*Tugui* acts on **edicts**, not on *zouchao*; the action for *zouchao* is "rebut and correct". I quoted the whole sentence and used only its second half, thereby attaching a downward-document action to an upward document. The same applies to *fengbo*: the "seal and return" half only works on downward edicts, so on v1's *zouchao* track that character was idling.

**Moving to the edict track erases all of this at once**: the agent drafts (the Secretariat drafter's role), you review, amend, and grant (the Chancellery's and the sovereign's roles), and the finalized text goes for execution. *Tugui*, *fengbo*, *huake*, and even the justification for the platform's name ("Tang edicts open with the characters 門下") **all land correctly**. The only cost is losing *zouchao* and *huawen* — and *huawen*'s Tang-era primary attestation turns out to be the weakest claim in the whole table (see below).

## One declaration: the role fold

**You are the emperor, seated in the Chancellery, personally performing the Supervising Secretary's duty.**

No such office ever existed. I state it explicitly so that it stands as a **deliberate convention of the naming system** rather than masquerading as a historical claim. The reason: in Tang practice "the Chancellery reviews" and "the sovereign grants" are two hands performing two steps, whereas here both are you pressing one button. Since the real topology is folded, the naming should reflect that rather than splitting you into two officers to please the histories.

**This single declaration resolves every actor mismatch at once**, which is why both of the words you chose survive:

- *Tugui* was properly a Supervising Secretary's act → you perform it in person; valid.
- *Pan* (判) was properly a Secretariat drafter's word (the six drafters each signing their own opinion) → same; valid.

Without this declaration, "I am the emperor" and "use 判" are two of your own annotations in direct conflict, and I will not quietly adopt both.

## Why this version has an evidence column

v1's "confidence" column was my own self-assessment, and the double review judged it worthless. I accept that: the confidence and the content came out of one generative process, so there is no independent signal. The evidence bore it out — a row marked "high" carried a cross-sourced quotation (the *New Tang History*'s wording attributed to the *Tang Liudian*), a row marked "medium" had its entire comparative argument backwards, while the "high" *tugui* row was in fact word-perfect. **"Confidence" meant "how familiar this feels", not "how much I checked".**

Worse: v1's only external check was ch. 4 of your *History of Chinese Official Titles* — **a textbook I wrote myself**. A model using its own prior output as external evidence can only catch "this memory contradicts that memory"; errors inside the shared prior are undetectable by construction. And that chapter presents exactly the simplified "Secretariat drafts → Chancellery rebuts → Department executes" diagram that produced the *huake* sequencing error: checking against it would not catch the error, it would reinforce it.

So from v2 onward: **the evidence column records what was checked and by whom, never how I feel.** Anything without a primary hit is downgraded to "received view" or "inferred".
